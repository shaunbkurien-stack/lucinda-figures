## Overall verdict

The reusable marine access concept is technically plausible and is the strongest current candidate for typical two-pile bents. It should proceed to supplier development and a two-set proving campaign.

The current evidence does not support a claim that two access sets can meet the end-2028 stress case. Two sets are the basic leapfrog and proving pair. The completed production fleet is an output of the measured cycle and dated calendar.

The current spreadsheet and authoring brief are suitable for Claude to prepare the next controlled Stage 1 draft and for Shaun and Jed to review the engineering basis. They are not cleared for STL issue, procurement, contractual action or cost commitment.

## Council lens 1: Marine construction and temporary works

### What holds up

- Two long-face floating platforms can remove repeated scaffold erection and dismantling from typical-bent access.
- Laterally travelling suspension is necessary because the 1:4 piles move outboard with depth.
- Traversing decks are the correct functional device for 360-degree pile access.
- An adjacent JUB is a credible campaign base for services, waste, welfare and crane support.

### What must be proven

- Certified platform mass, centre of gravity, buoyancy, freeboard, deck load and lifting points.
- Winch WLL, speed, duty, synchronisation, fault response and emergency/manual lowering.
- Sliding-clamp travel and locking.
- Platform states: afloat, hook-on, transition, suspended, lowest elevation, fault, survival and recovery.
- Traversing-deck load path between two independently suspended platforms.
- Half-shell alignment and interlock tolerance.
- Service umbilical travel, support and emergency disconnect.
- JUB bathymetry, leg penetration, crane reach, deck layout, coverage per set-up and cyclone refuge.

Council conclusion: technically plausible, not yet engineered.

## Council lens 2: Structural repair, blasting, coating and jackets

### Current evidence

- Weld preparation and welding claimed to July 2026 total approximately 473.025 lm.
- Expressed against 9.0 inspection-stage equivalents, this is approximately 52.6 lm per bent equivalent.
- 10 mm repair work forms approximately 61% of the measured weld quantity, which is materially heavier than the scheduled 8 mm basis.
- Two upper pile bands are approximately 27 to 29 m2. The full typical-bent blast area including headstock is approximately 46 to 51 m2.

### Required sequence

1. Remove wrapping, clean, inspect and UT.
2. Diagnostic blast and mark-up both piles and the headstock.
3. Issue one bent-level repair package if accepted by the reviewing engineer.
4. Complete welding, NDT and final surface preparation.
5. Complete coat 1, stripe coats, wash and thorough drying, recoat verification, coat 2 and QA.
6. Complete jackets, including the below-LAT method once established.
7. Complete headstock and interface work.
8. Count the bent only after all QA records are accepted.

### Open gates

- Headstock containment configuration.
- Below-LAT jacket installation and lower termination.
- Accepted weld output including fit-up, NDT and rework.
- Safe simultaneous weld zones on one access set.
- Coating elapsed work-lot, not only active spray hours.

Council conclusion: the trade scope is understood, but the complete elapsed cycle is not yet measured.

## Council lens 3: Planning and productivity

### Current-method baseline

- Safe access: 7.9 bent equivalents over approximately 21.9 calendar weeks, about 0.36 equivalents/week.
- Inspection and UT: about 0.41 equivalents/week.
- Blast and coating: about 0.32 equivalents/week.
- Jackets: about 0.55 pile equivalents/week.
- These are payment-stage quantities, not QA-closed bents.
- Representative current bent cycle: approximately 14 to 15 calendar days, with a 3-day approval hold and repeated access handling.

### Required stress case

- 227 bents over two nominal 30-week seasons: 3.783 QA-closed bents/week.
- The 227 quantity is an unverified whole-scope stress case pending the current STL bent register.

### Proposed incomplete model

Current represented access-set occupancy: approximately 14.02 shifts, including 1.10 shifts relocation.

At six days/week and 85% residual stochastic availability:

| Complete access sets | Modelled QA-closed bents/week |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.73 |
| 3 | 1.09 |
| 4 | 1.46 |
| 5 | 1.82 |

Diagnostic sensitivity at the 3.783-bent/week stress rate:

| Scenario | Cycle, days | Sets required |
|---|---:|---:|
| S1 base incomplete | 14.02 | 11 |
| S2 four weld crews at 8 lm/crew-shift | 11.26 | 9 |
| S3 plus no routine approval hold | 8.26 | 7 |
| S4 six crews at 10 lm/crew-shift and no routine hold | 7.48 | 6 |

These figures are not recommended fleets. Headstock containment, below-LAT jackets, containment clean-out/rework and coating elapsed holds remain outside the completed cycle.

Council conclusion: two sets are a proving pair. The end-2028 production configuration remains unproven.

## Council lens 4: Estimating, commercial and contract

### Safe commercial statements

- The current contract contains an earlier access-cost basis of approximately AUD 9.275 million ex GST for the firm scope on the reconciled basis.
- This is not the remaining avoidable cost, termination value or current cost to complete.
- The proposed model requires a bottom-up build-up covering engineering, mobilisation, platforms, containment, JUB, workboats, labour, accommodation, materials, waste, seasonal treatment, proving and contingency.
- No cost saving should be promised until current and proposed cost-to-complete values use the same remaining scope, calendar and price date.

### Required market evidence

- Written GGC platform and containment offer.
- Platform configuration, mass, capacities, winches, modifications, availability, rates and exclusions.
- Candidate JUB and workboat rates and availability.
- Labour, accommodation, fuel, waste and material rates.
- Current committed and remaining AAB cost.

### Contract boundary

Endura is the owner-side methodology adviser. The Stage 1 report may compare methods, programme, cost and risk. It should not direct contract termination. STL and its legal/contractual advisers retain that decision.

Council conclusion: the cost model structure is complete; the estimate is incomplete until written inputs are loaded.

## Council lens 5: STL technical evaluator

A capable STL reviewer is likely to challenge:

1. Why two sets are shown if the model indicates six to eleven sets.
2. Whether the JUB can service and recover the full fleet.
3. Whether the headstock can be contained without returning to full-bent scaffold.
4. How below-LAT jackets are installed and accepted.
5. Whether one bent-level approval is acceptable.
6. Whether 53 lm per bent is representative or distorted by the current work package.
7. How coating recoat time is represented in the line-of-balance.
8. How the system applies to anchor, passing-bay, special and EJ bents.
9. How ship calls and cyclone recovery affect the dated programme.
10. Whether the proposed cost includes the full transition and proving burden.

The report will be credible if it answers these directly and leaves unproven values as gates. It will lose credibility if it presents two sets, one shift or a 3.7-bent/week rate as already demonstrated.

## Final council position

The concept is worth progressing. The proposal should sell the engineering pathway and the evidence-led proving strategy, not an unsupported production promise. The strongest case to STL is that the current method has a demonstrated serial-cycle problem, the marine system removes a material portion of that problem, and Endura has defined the exact supplier, engineering, trial, programme and commercial gates needed to turn the concept into a defensible execution recommendation.
