## Council outcome

### Status: suitable for Shaun and Jed internal review, not cleared for STL issue

The draft now answers the Stage 1 proposal at concept-assessment level. It includes the constraint envelope, option screening, preferred concept, detailed methodology, productivity comparison, indicative programme, parametric cost model, multi-criteria assessment, supplier position, proving campaign and implementation roadmap.

The package deliberately does not claim that the preferred concept has demonstrated the end-2028 outcome. That would not be supported by the evidence currently available.

## Lens 1: Principal decommissioning and marine construction

What holds:

- The typical-bent concept is physically coherent at concept level.
- The sequence distinguishes floating, transition and suspended work states.
- The platform set remains at the bent through the full accepted scope.
- The JUB is a support base rather than the workface.
- A hybrid method is retained for non-typical bent families.

Challenges carried forward:

- Certified platform mass, buoyancy and centre of gravity.
- Sliding suspension and winch control.
- Traversing-deck load path between independently suspended platforms.
- Half-shell alignment, seal and relative movement.
- Headstock enclosure geometry.
- Jacket installation below LAT.
- JUB reach, seabed and service umbilicals.
- Full-fleet cyclone recovery.

Council position: technically plausible, not yet engineered.

## Lens 2: Planning and productivity

What holds:

- The current programme and current claim quantities are kept on separate measurement bases.
- The report does not divide 3.78 completed bents/week by 0.36 stage equivalents/week.
- The current planned in-season rate is independently stated as approximately 2.46 completed bents/week.
- The required whole-scope stress rate is 3.783 completed bents/week.
- S1 to S4 are transparent mathematical sensitivities rather than a recommended fleet.

Disconfirming result:

- The incomplete model requires 11 sets in the base case and six sets only in the strongest sensitivity.
- Headstock containment, coating elapsed holds, below-LAT jackets and closeout/rework remain outside the completed cycle.
- The final required fleet could therefore be larger or the completion date later.

Council position: the concept can outperform the repeated scaffold cycle, but the current evidence does not prove the end-2028 whole-scope stress case.

## Lens 3: Commercial and estimating

What holds:

- Cost is built by fixed, time-related, set-related and production-variable activities.
- Current contract access values are not treated as the remaining avoidable cost.
- Every proposed monetary rate is visibly classified.
- Supplier rates can replace working allowances without rebuilding the model.

Challenge:

- No structured written GGC/JUB/containment quotation was verified in the organised package.
- The current scenario totals are working allowances, not a supplier-priced estimate.
- Remaining scope by bent family is not known.
- The estimate is not suitable for budget commitment.

Council position: the cost structure is fit for market sounding and option comparison, not commercial approval.

## Lens 4: Contracts, compliance and environmental assurance

What holds:

- The report remains owner-side advice.
- It does not recommend termination or award.
- The contract scope, shipping interface, make-safe requirement and wet-season recovery are visible.
- The waste and containment requirements are incorporated.

Open gates:

- GBRMPA, MSQ, Port of Townsville, environmental and biosecurity positions.
- Vessel classification and marine insurance.
- Noise and working-hour restrictions.
- Dangerous-waste classification, transport and disposal acceptance.
- Written responsibility matrix for STL, contractor, suppliers and reviewing engineer.

Council position: suitable for methodology direction, not tender issue.

## Lens 5: Client technical evaluator

Likely client challenges:

1. Why should STL believe the proposed fleet can be mobilised and operated safely at the scale shown?
2. How will the headstock be contained?
3. How will the jacket be installed and inspected below LAT?
4. Can one bent-level repair approval genuinely be achieved?
5. What supplier equipment and rates are actually available?
6. How does the JUB operate through the shallow section?
7. How is the full fleet recovered for a cyclone?
8. What is the final remaining priority quantity?

The draft answers those questions honestly by converting them into acceptance gates. It must not be edited to hide them.

## Final council direction

Use the report and model for the Shaun/Jed review and for a structured discussion with STL.

Before external issue:

1. Insert the current STL bent register.
2. Replace working commercial allowances with written supplier quotations.
3. Add bathymetry, metocean and the dated shipping calendar.
4. Obtain a supplier headstock containment concept.
5. Close the below-LAT jacket method.
6. Confirm the bent-level approval strategy.
7. Develop the RPEQ temporary-works basis.
8. Run the proving campaign model and update the line of balance.
9. Perform a new independent Full Review because the source and engineering basis will have materially changed.

No statement in the current package should be read as professional certification, construction approval, procurement approval or completion guarantee.
